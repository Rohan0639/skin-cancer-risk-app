Skin Lesion Risk Screening

Upload a phone photo of a skin lesion and a few details. Get a risk level, a heatmap of what the model looked at, and a plain-language explanation built on the dermatological ABCDE rule.

Try the live demo Source on GitHub Model weights
⚠️ This is not a medical device. It has not been clinically validated, it does not provide a diagnosis, and its output must never be used to rule out skin cancer. If you are worried about a mark on your skin — or it is changing, bleeding, itching, or not healing — see a doctor regardless of what this tool says.

How it works

Eight stages, in a deliberate order. The quality gate runs first, because a confident-looking number computed from an unusable photo is the worst possible output for a tool like this.

1

Quality gate

Blur, exposure, contrast, resolution and framing. A bad photo gets a retake prompt, not a risk score.

2

Segmentation

Flat-fielded Otsu threshold with GrabCut refinement, so uneven flash lighting can't swallow the lesion.

3

PanDerm features

A dermatology foundation model (ViT-B/16) turns the photo into a 768-dimensional embedding.

4

Metadata fusion

Age, sex and body site are encoded and fused with the image embedding before classification.

5

Grad-CAM

Shows where the model actually looked — and flags when that was no better than chance.

6

ABCDE

Asymmetry, border, colour, diameter and evolving, measured classically and independently of the network.

7

Change detection

Compares against an earlier photo, correcting for camera distance, angle and lighting colour.

8

One report

A single recommendation, downloadable as Markdown, HTML or JSON.

Explainability

Two independent explanations, deliberately. The heatmap shows what the network keyed on; the ABCDE measurements come from classical computer vision and never consult the network. When they disagree, the report says so rather than inventing a justification for the model.

“Not measurable” is reported, never hidden. A photo carries no scale, so Diameter is only scored if you tell the app how wide the frame is in real life. Evolving needs an earlier photo. Both are shown as ? rather than quietly counted as reassuring — an absent warning is not the same as a clean result.

Risk levels

The source datasets' diagnosis labels are collapsed into three triage levels. The mapping lives in an editable config file, not in code.

LevelCoversRecommendation
Low Ordinary nevus, seborrheic keratosis, dermatofibroma, vascular lesions Keep monitoring; re-photograph in ~3 months
Moderate Actinic keratosis and other pre-malignant lesions Book a non-urgent appointment
High Melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma See a dermatologist promptly

Advice only ever escalates. Low model confidence, a detected change, three or more ABCDE flags, or a heatmap that missed the lesion each push the reported level one step towards seeing a doctor — never away.

Honest limitations

  • No validated performance numbers. The classifier on this demo was trained on synthetic images to verify the pipeline. It demonstrates that the system works end to end; it is not a measured clinical result, and the app labels its samples as synthetic.
  • Skin-tone bias. The dermatology datasets this design targets over-represent lighter skin. Expect worse performance on darker skin. Nothing here corrects for that.
  • Three levels are a triage simplification, not a clinical grading system. Collapsing seven diagnoses into three discards information a dermatologist would use.
  • Segmentation is classical, not learned. It works on centred, well-lit photos of a single lesion and degrades on cluttered ones, heavy hair, or multiple nearby lesions.
